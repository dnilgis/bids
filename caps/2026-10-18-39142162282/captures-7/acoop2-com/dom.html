 10.16.2026

 

Happy Friday. Futures markets ended the week Friday much the same way as how they started it on Monday, with long liquidation a feature all week amid the USDA's loosening of balance sheets a week ago and there being little offsetting bullish input that ensued. Charts have become oversold in recent days as the selling pressed on, but we maintain that the bull camp is going to need some sort of new input to push prices back higher with the funds now coming out of their long position a bit and with US crop sizes generally known in another few weeks. The transition from US crop focus to South American crop focus will remain ongoing.

 

🌽 Corn Market Update

 

📈 Prices:

  • December Corn (CZ): $4.74 1/4, down 3 3/4 cents
  • March Corn (CH): $4.86 1/2, down 4 1/2 cents
  • Dec/Mar Spread (CZ/CH): -12 1/4, up 3/4 cent
  • For the week: CZ was down 5 1/2 cents; CH was down 7 3/4 cents

 

📋 Market Headlines

  • The USDA's daily sales reporting system this morning showed private exporters sold 127,000 MTs of corn to Japan for delivery during the 2026/27 marketing year. Of note, this is the first corn flash to a destination not named Mexico or unknown since a sale to Colombia on July 20th.

 

  • Weekly export sales data this morning for the week ending October 8th showed corn exports in the week at 1.122 MMTs, with featured buyers in the week being Mexico (544,500 MTs), Japan (220,100 MTs), and South Korea (199,900 MTs). Cumulative sales now stand at 20.666 MMTs, which is down 33% from last year.

 

  • Weekly Commitment of Traders data from the CFTC this week showed managed money traders in the week ending October 13th were sellers of 86,481 contracts of corn, making them now net-long 230,877 contracts. Funds sold 120k+ contracts in June of this year, with the biggest week of selling on record seen in February of 2023 at more than 147k contracts.

 

Summary:

Corn futures fell to new lows for the week on Friday, taking out the bottom end of the range seen the rest of the week this week but still holding above the report day low from last Friday. Inversely, cash markets showed some strength the back half of the week, with farmers still opting to push for bean harvest where able and as rains slowed those who had finished bean harvest already the last couple days. That said though, as dryness shows back up next week, its expected that a sizeable chunk of the crop is going to come across the scales in the next ten days, which could lead to increased farmer hedge selling. Aside from that, it will be South American weather and the prospects for Chinese buying that remain the loudest and will have the biggest impact on price direction longer term.

 

 

🌱 Soybean Market Update

 

📈 Prices:

  • November Soybeans (SX): $12.61 1/4, down 1 3/4 cents
  • January Soybeans (SF): $12.75 3/4, down 2 cents
  • December Soybean Meal (MZ): $357.10, down $2.70/ton
  • December Soybean Oil (LZ): 67.13, down 0.02 cents/lb
  • Nov/Jan Spread (SX/SF): -14 1/2, up 1/4 cent
  • For the week: SX was down 30 3/4 cents; SF was down 33 cents; MZ was down $8.50/ton; LZ was down 0.89 cents/lb
  • Private exporters this morning also sold 558,000 MTs of soybeans for delivery to China during the 2026/27 marketing year, as well as 236,000 MTs of soybeans for delivery to unknown destinations during the 2026/27 marketing year. Soybean flashes to China this week totaled 1.358 MMTs, with another 519,450 MTs to unknown.

 

📋 Market Headlines:

 

  • This morning's weekly export sales report for the week ending October 8th showed soybean sales in the week at 575k MTs, with featured buyers in the week being China (126,300 MTs), Egypt (110,700 MTs), Bangladesh (110,000 MTs) and Japan (109,600 MTs); unknown destinations assigned out/canceled 78,700 MTs in the week. Cumulative sales now stand at 23.360 MMTs, which is up 74% from last year.

 

  • This afternoon's Commitment of Traders update from the CFTC showed managed money traders in the week ending October 13th were sellers of 14,169 contracts of soybeans, making them now net-long 237,491 contracts. In the products, funds sold 672 contracts of meal (+206,131) and sold 10,119 contracts of oil (+89,328).

 

Summary:

Soybean futures made it a perfect five for five to the downside this week, scoring new lows for the move for the second straight day and closing lower on what continues to be long liquidation by the funds and hedge selling by the farmer. Chinese buying has remained steady, but traders are aware that there's a cap on this current run of purchases and are curious as to what happens with business beyond the first of the new year. Industry sources also estimate upwards of a quarter of the crop could've been harvested over the last week to 10 days, and if just half of this was sold across the scales it would more than offset the couple million tons of purchases done by the Chinese. Usage remains good and is up from a year ago, but a record crop in Brazil followed by a record crop in the US will go a long way towards satisfying that demand.

 

 

🌾 Wheat Market Update

 

📈 Prices:

  • December Chicago Wheat (WZ): $6.60 1/2, down 9 1/2 cents
  • March Chicago Wheat (WH): $6.75, down 9 1/4 cents
  • Dec/Mar Spread (WZ/WH): -14 1/2, down 1/4 cent
  • For the week: WZ was down 10 1/2 cents; WH was down 10 1/4 cents
  • Weekly export sales data for the week ending October 8th showed wheat sales in the week at 320k MTs, with featured buyers in the week being Taiwan (105,800 MTs) and Sri Lanka (68,100 MTs). Cumulative sales now stand at 10.429 MMTs, which is down 33% from last year.

 

📋 Market Headlines:

 

  • Weekly Commitment of Traders data from the CFTC for the week ending October 13th showed managed money traders in the week were sellers of 3,495 contracts of Chicago wheat, making them now net-short 33,038 contracts.

 

Summary:

Wheat futures led the push to the downside on Friday, scoring new lows both for the week and the larger down move on a continued lack of buying interest and as global fund traders seem to continue to be interested in using US wheat futures as the short side of hedges both against futures in Europe and also against broader length in the rest of the ag space. We talked about it earlier this week, but cash markets in the EU are just barely starting to show signs of tightening due to the ongoing situation in the Black Sea. However, tighter markets across the pond are still notably cheaper than export offers from the US, and until this changes, its probably difficult for the US market to see a ton of upside price movement. Charts are becoming oversold like the rest of the space, but we don't see a lot of reason for a bounce in prices otherwise short term.

  • Outside markets were sharply mixed to end the week:

  • Stock index futures: The Dow Jones index is down 320 points, the S&P500 index is down 100 points, and the NASDAQ is down 820 points

🌦️ Weather Outlook

 

 

⚡ Short-term Forecast:

  • Weekend weather, while cold across the Midwest, looks to be conducive for good harvest activity as there is little in the way of precip forecast into and through the week next week. Frost risks will dip well into the southern Plains tonight and into tomorrow, but the cold doesn't look to be long-lived with daytime highs expected to return to warmer than normal next week.

 

📆 Extended Forecast:

  • The dryness stays in the forecast then into and through the week next week, with high pressure expected to linger over the east-central US while low pressure systems impact both the East and West Coasts. Week two forecasts have monsoonal rains then opening back up through the southwest and into the mid-section of the country which likely impact the western Corn Belt again, but the central and east parts still have little precip seen in the period.

 

🗺️ South America Forecast:

  • Precip maps for South America trended wetter the last 24 hours across the southern eastern and regions of Brazil, but were little changed across central regions as near-normal rainfall is expected to continue into next week. Rains will be spottier in west-central Brazil, but there continues to be little if any concern in the forecast today with planting pace at or ahead of average pace.
