Grain Gleanings

 

 

Mobile App

By Jeff Moritz, Lead Grain Merchandiser

The great weather these past several days has put soybean harvest into full gear. While yields are off from last year, in talking to a lot of you, it is a tribute to current farming practices and seed technology that yield returns better than expectations in late August.

There will be another USDA WASDE report published this Friday with many in the trade focused on the production side of the Corn and Soybean Balance Sheets. According to the analysts, Corn production is expected to slide to 15.721 billion bushels, 177.8 bu/acre yield. Corn carryout is expected to increase due to the bearish surprise increase in old crop stocks in the Quarterly Stocks Report last Wednesday. Soybean production is expected to increase fractionally to 52.9 bu/acre yield, with production at 4.534 billion bushels. Soybean carryout is expected to be essentially unchanged.

Studying the price charts, Soybeans feel like they may be in consolidation mode for a bit trading either side of $13.00 November Futures and is currently oversold. The market needs some fresh news that would have to come from additional Chinese purchases beyond the 25MMT trade agreement. Weekly soybean export sales were a bit underwhelming, largely due to China's Golden Week holiday. However, year-to-date soybean export sales remain strong, nearly double last year's pace, as China continues purchasing toward trade targets. At this current time, they are over halfway to their current goal. However, underwhelming outcomes from a recent U.S.-China trade summit have introduced some uncertainty for additional demand. Corn seems to be taking a cue from Soybeans as it continues to consolidate around the $5.00 December Futures area and it is oversold technically. Weekly export sales were disappointing this week as well. Export sales for the marketing year are down about 30% compared to this time last year. A strengthening U.S. Dollar has functioned as a significant headwind, making U.S. grain less competitive globally.

With harvest in full swing, I would like to take the opportunity to highlight a great tool that we offer in the form of the CFC App. Many of you are using it and that is great, but for those of you that have not had the chance, I would invite you to download it today!

You will have access to scale tickets, contracts, bushel balances, cash bids, and market information— all from your smartphone.

You will be able to access scale tickets virtually in real time, which allows you to know how many bushels you have delivered and how much that still needs to be delivered. The Scale Ticket interface is easy to understand and tracks grade factors such as Moisture and Test Weight.

You will also have access to your contracts that you have with any CFC location. You will be able to see the status of any contract to find out whether it is filled or is still open.

The App will also provide you with real-time bushel balances of your grain across all CFC locations.

You can also submit price offers through the app to your local CFC location at any time of day.

The App will also provide delayed cash bids for all of our CFC locations. You will also be able to access Grains, Livestock and Ethanol Futures from CME or MGEX.

You can find the App on Google Play for Android devices or the App Store for iPhones by searching for Central Farmers. If you have trouble getting into the App, please contact your CFC originator and they will be happy to assist. Download it today.

We hope you have a great and safe rest of your harvest. Thanks again for your business!

Happy Pumpkin Month

By Becky Johnson, Elevator Location Manager, Salem

We found out that it really can rain.  It may not have been ideal timing, but the rain was needed to improve the soil for next year and help reduce the fire risk.  Looks like the ten-day forecast shows the rain will be gone and at least partly sunny days lay ahead.

I know all are anxious to get in the field and start harvesting. I want to remind everyone of a few safety tips in hopes of preventing/reducing the risk of accidents.

  1. Get plenty of rest. Fatigue is a leading cause inotherwise preventable accidents.
  2. Maintain a nutritious diet.
  3. Drink lots of water to stay hydrated.
  4. Take care of yourself. Mental health is as important as physical health.
  5. Keep equipment maintained and inspected.
  6. Make sure guards and shields are properly attached to equipment.
  7. Make sure equipment is properly lit and slow-moving signs are attached and visible.
  8. Take time to train others helping you on your equipment.
  9. Be alert to where others are and always check underneath and around equipment before moving it.
  10. Wear PPE especially High Vis with reflective tape during dark hours.
  11. Practice bin safety.
  12. Beware of machinery entanglement.
  13. Have an emergency plan prepared in case someone does not show up at a given time or you cannot get ahold of them, so you know where to start looking for them at.
  14. Travel safely. Limit distractions and follow the rules of the road.

Have a safe harvest and as always thank you for your patronage.

Harvest Delays

By Matt Morog, Grain Department Manager

Corn and soybean world balance sheets continue to aid in the strength of the current prices. Global corn demand remains strong and is now lacking the movement from the Black Sea necessary to fill all slots. On top of this the US has trimmed its ending stock estimates. Fund traders have become record long corn futures, which has me nervous as this aids in the stairs up elevator down chart we have seen in the past. Yields locally are going to make marketing grain difficult. It’s difficult to know how much higher we go from here. We haven’t hit any bearish cards for a few weeks and it’s showing. I fear if we turn over a card and it is bearish we are going to dip hard. It’s hard to estimate how low the funds are determining US yields. Once we get rolling will they get less nervous and sell off their length? Hedging corn as we go here down the strip might not be a bad idea for most. I know there are many different circumstances on an individual level, but the market doesn’t determine that.

Soybeans overall are looking at a bit larger US crop than last year, but we also have Chinese demand. China so far is on its way to booking the 25 mmt it committed. Soybean crush isn’t $4 anymore, but it’s still very ripe at over $2. This isn’t going to slow down crush demand anytime too soon, but the lack of availability of soybeans on the front end might. Again, I continue to think like corn, that hedging percentages of your soybean crop as you can determine yield is the prudent option. Similar to corn, we haven’t seen a bearish card flip over in a while and we are likely due one.

Every one of our facilities is ready to go for harvest, we just need the forecast to dry out. Our harvest storage program for both corn and soybeans is going to be 10 days free followed by 7c per month. Everyone in the area is about 6 to 8c per month on storage with some of the crush plants using minimums to the first of the year. We are going to do what we can to take all the wet corn we can, but we can only dry so much so fast. Our abilities to dry will be tested I assume.

I hope we all have a very safe upcoming harvest.
