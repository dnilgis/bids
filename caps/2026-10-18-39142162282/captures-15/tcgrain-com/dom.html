Commentary

10/16/26:

Corn: Corn futures continued to weaken, with December corn closing overnight at $4.76¼, a new settlement low since mid-August. The market’s price trend remains negative even though export demand was solid. Weekly corn sales reached 1.122 million metric tons, near the upper end of expectations, led by Mexico, Japan, South Korea, Guatemala, and Italy. Buyers are still taking U.S. corn, but that demand has not been strong enough to stop speculative funds from selling.  

Wheat: Winter wheat was hit harder, falling about 6 to 7 cents overnight. Black Sea fighting is no longer giving prices much support because the market already knows about it, while favorable Australian crop prospects and improved soil moisture in U.S. winter-wheat areas are raising expectations for production. Hard red spring wheat held up somewhat better, but its overall price trend also remains weak. Export sales totaled 319,900 metric tons, within expectations, with hard red spring wheat accounting for the largest share.

The takeaway is that wheat has respectable export business, but traders are currently more focused on adequate supplies, improved planting conditions, and funds exiting long positions, all of which are keeping pressure on prices.
